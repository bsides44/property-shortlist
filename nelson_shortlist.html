    Where the numbers come from

    Hazards are point-in-polygon queries against Nelson City Council's published hazard services — river flood depth (present day and 2130), coastal flood depth, wave run-up, coastal erosion, liquefaction, slope instability tiers, and the fault deformation overlay. Depth bands and probabilities are the council's own wording.

    Decade built, capital value and last sale come from council rating records via homes.co.nz. Beds, baths, floor and land area are as advertised on Trade Me.

    Distances are straight-line to OpenStreetMap points, not walking or driving time.

    What this doesn't tell you

    A property outside every hazard polygon is outside these polygons. It is not a clean geotech report, and it says nothing about overland flow, stormwater capacity, or what a specific site's ground actually does.

    Trade Me plots listings to the street address, so a point can sit a few metres off. On a boundary, check the council viewer directly before drawing conclusions.

    Coastal and 2130 river layers are climate projections under a stated scenario, not forecasts.

    Adding another region

    Coverage here stops at the Nelson City Council boundary. Each council publishes its own hazard services, so a new region means pointing the same query at a new endpoint and mapping its layer names into the same severity scale.

    For your Wellington lists that means Greater Wellington Regional Council plus the relevant city council; for Richmond and Best Island, Tasman District Council.
